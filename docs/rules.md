# Design Principles
- **Fiction first** → The goal is to tell an interesting story. The rules and dice only exist to fuel the narrative. When they don't make sense, use rulings over rules: common sense, rule of cool, rule of fun.
- **Classless** → Characters express their identity through their gear, traits, and choices.
- **Diegetic growth** → Characters improve through failure, hardship, survival and investment.
- **Player Choice** → Players understand the stakes of their choices, and risks are always made clear.

# Principles for GMs
- Telegraph danger, create real risks, give opportunities to interact with the world.
- Describe consistently, provide useful information, be helpful, give honest and direct answers.
- Characters know what they know, and see what they see. Don't withhold information.
- Convey the NPC's wants and needs clearly, let roleplay and player creativity push conversations forward. Only use social rolls when conversation alone can't settle the outcome.

# Characters
All characters have:
- 4 Attributes, each with an associated die:
    - Strength (STR) → might, vigor, resilience, stamina
    - Agility (AGI) → speed, reflexes, precision, coordination
    - Focus (FCS) → clarity, senses, willpower, attunement
    - Presence (PRE) → aura, intuition, empathy, composure
- Traits → Narrative keywords or phrases describing a character's skills, bonds, personality, physique, or background. Each trait has an associated die.
- Gear → 10 slots for weapons, tools, items and equipment. Each may have an associated die, an Armor value when worn, or special properties.
- Pockets → For carrying items of negligible encumbrance, such as a trinket, a handful of coins, a key, a letter.
- Armor → Passive damage reduction. Its value is subtracted from incoming damage.
- Stress → Slots marked when suffering pain, strain, fatigue, mental or physical burden.
- Wounds → Slots filled when suffering major injuries.
- Mortal Wound → A slot filled when suffering a critical injury.
- Major Damage Threshold → Damage ≥ this number causes a wound.
- Critical Damage Threshold → Damage ≥ this number causes a mortal wound.

Player characters also have:
- Resolve → A resource used to influence rolls and trigger special abilities. You can have a maximum of 6.

## Character Creation
Starting characters have:
- Choose one of these attribute layouts: [d6,d6,d6,d6], [d4,d6,d6,d8], [d4,d4,d8,d8], [d4,d4,d6,d10]. (In procedural terms, start with all d6, then you may drop one in size to raise another, twice.) Assign each die to one attribute in any order.
- Choose 2 starting Traits:
    - A profession **d8** → A skill or domain you are trained in. (*Soldier / Blacksmith / Apothecary*)
    - A catchphrase **d6** → A broad, short and thematic sentence. (*Can't catch me / Just one more / I see you*)
- Choose your starting Gear.
- Armor from your choice of gear: Clothes (0), Light Armor (1), Medium Armor (2), Heavy Armor (3).
- 6 empty Stress slots.
- Major Damage Threshold of 8
- Critical Damage Threshold of 16
- 2 empty Wound slots and 1 empty Mortal Wound slot.
- 2/6 Resolve.

# The Spotlight
Think of the game flow like a movie. The spotlight indicates the narrative focus, where the camera is currently pointing. When you have the spotlight, you get to move and act for a Sequence.

# Time
Gameplay is divided into Sequences. The duration of a Sequence in the fiction changes with the situation:
- A phase during Downtime
- A few hours during Travel
- A few minutes during Exploration
- A few seconds during Encounters
- A split-second during a Reaction

A Day has 3 Phases:
- Morning → Sunrise to Noon
- Afternoon → Noon to Sunset
- Night → Sunset to Sunrise

## Omens
Omens are the GM's resource. They mirror the player's Resolve.
The GM can have a maximum of 12 Omens. The GM starts with X+1d4 Omens, where X is the number of player characters.
The GM can spend an Omen at any time to take the spotlight and make a GM Move.
When the GM has the spotlight, they can keep spending Omens to make additional GM Moves until they choose to relinquish it or run out of Omens.

### GM Strategy Advice
- Use Omen deliberately to create tension, challenge, and drive the narrative forward.
- Don't hoard Omen. The cap exists for a reason. A full pool of Omen is a storm cloud for the players. Wait for the right moment, and unleash.
- If you run low, keep some Omens in reserve for reactions and unexpected developments. 
- Omen and Resolve create a natural push-and-pull, ebb and flow of narrative control between the GM and the players.

## GM Moves
Under the spotlight, the GM makes GM Moves, including but not limited to:
- Make an NPC act, attack or react
- Introduce a complication, limit, cost, or consequence to the players' action
- Escalate the stakes
- Introduce hazards
- Reveal information

# Dice
The game uses dice of various sizes, typically d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, and exceptionally d20 and d100.

## When to roll
Roll when a character attempts an action for which the outcome is uncertain.
Consider these 3 factors:
- **Time** - Can you do this without rushing?
- **Mastery** - Do you have the necessary skills?
- **Gear** - Do you have the necessary equipment/materials?

If you have all three, you likely automatically succeed.
If one is missing, you may need to roll to determine the outcome.
If two or more are missing, you probably can't do it.

## What to roll
Before rolling, assemble a pool of dice. Any relevant dice may contribute including:
- One Attribute die
- Up to 2 involved Gear dice
- One relevant Trait die
- Situational advantage
- Ally help dice

## How to read the dice
Every roll has a target difficulty T. By default, T is equal to 8 unless specified otherwise.

After rolling, sort the dice, then read:
- **Result** → the sum of the 2 highest dice. (If only one die is rolled, use that die.)
- **Edges** → any other dice showing 4 or higher, beyond the two highest.

The roll succeeds if the result reaches T or higher.
Consider granting partial success if the result reaches T-2 or T-1.
On T above 8, if the roll fails with Edges, consider letting the player "buy" a partial success in exchange for all Edges.

## Difficulty Reference
- T=6 — Easy
- T=8 — Moderate (default, most common)
- T=10 — Hard
- T=12 — Very Hard
- T=14 — Insane
- T=16 — Impossible

## Dice Reference
- d4 — Novice — 1 in 4 odds of 4+
- d6 — Adept — 1 in 2
- d8 — Advanced — 5 in 8
- d10 — Expert — 7 in 10
- d12 — Master — 3 in 4
- d20 — Legend — 17 in 20
- d100 — Cosmic — 95 in 100

As you progress, your rolls become more reliable and impactful.
More dice mean less swing, bigger dice mean better odds and greater potential.

## Opposition & Saves
An opposition is a roll made to mitigate another roll. The opposition's result is subtracted from the original roll's.
A save is a roll made to resist or avoid an effect or consequence. The save's result must reach a set T to succeed.

## Action rolls
Anytime a character attempts an action that has an uncertain outcome, they must make an action roll.

### Player Action rolls
Actions rolls made by player characters have additional mechanics:
- Regardless of success, look at the parity of the Result:
    - Odd Result → The GM gains 1 Omen.
    - Even Result → The PC gains 1 Resolve.
- If the roll is a failure, partial success, or odd, the GM gets the spotlight to apply the consequence, cost, or limits.

**Only** player action rolls generate resources and GM spotlight in this way.

## Attack rolls
An attack roll is a type of Action roll made when a character attempts to harm another. Attack rolls have the following properties:
- Attack rolls have no difficulty. An unopposed melee attack always lands.
- Attack rolls can be reduced by Reaction rolls.
- An attack roll is considered a failure if it fails to deal any damage. Any attack reduced to 0 by armor or other mitigation counts as a failure.
- By default, the damage of an attack is the Result of the roll.
- When a group attacks a single target, they roll all their dice as one pool.
- When attacking with multiple weapons, each weapon's die contributes to the pool.

### Ranged attacks
Ranged attacks have their damage reduced by distance and cover. 
- Within effective range → no reduction
- Beyond effective range → -2
- Beyond 2× effective range → -4
- Target behind partial cover → -4
- Target behind full cover → -8

## Reaction rolls
When the scene presents imminent danger or harm with a split-second to react, characters may make a Reaction roll.
Making a reaction roll costs 1 Resolve for PCs (unless the GM requests the roll), or 1 Omen for GM-run NPCs.
Generally, Reaction rolls are opposition or save rolls.

Reaction rolls never generate resources or trigger GM moves.

# Encounters
High stakes situations where characters are under pressure.
When you take a Sequence, you may, always in that order:
- Take a Move Action
- Take a Main Action
- Take sub-action bought with Edges from your action roll

## Move Action
You move up to X meters, where X is the size of your Agility die.

## Main Action
A main action includes:
- Dash (Move an additional X meters, but you yield the spotlight to the GM)
- Attack
- Cast a spell
- Use an item
- Hide
- Interact with the environment
- Anything that can be done **in a few seconds**, according to the fiction

If you attempt an action from a disadvantageous position, the GM may require you to downgrade your roll.

## Edge bonus
When your action roll yields Edges, you may perform one additional sub-action per Edge. Each sub-action can only be performed once per sequence.
- Move → Reposition or disengage, taking a move action again after your action / act quickly and quietly
- Impose → Create a tag in the scene / Give a target a condition
- Setup → You or your ally get an advantage on the next related action
- Guard → The next attack or consequence against you or your ally has reduced effect
- Read → You learn information about the scene / enemies / weak points
- Trigger → Activate your gear's Edge ability
- Other effect of a similar level of impact

## Reaction
In response to another incoming harm, a character can take a **Reaction**. Reactions include:
- Dodging
- Blocking
- Parrying
- Counterattacking
- Anything that can be done **in a split second**, according to the fiction

If you attempt something slow or shaky, the GM may require you to downgrade your roll.
Reactions are declared before rolling. Both parties assemble their pools openly, and roll at the same time. You can tell how dangerous something coming at you is, just not how bad it's going to be yet.

# Resolve Moves
All player characters can spend Resolve to:
- **Push** → Before you roll, you may spend 1 Resolve to add a copy of the biggest die in your pool, once per roll. You can only duplicate your intrinsic dice (Attribute, Gear, Traits), not dice added from other sources (Advantage, Vulnerability, Help). 
- **Reaction** → After incoming harm is declared, you may spend 1 Resolve to make a reaction roll.

You may acquire additional resolve moves through character advancement or special abilities.

# Damage
When you take damage, first subtract your armor from the incoming damage, unless the attack specifically bypasses armor.
Then, compare the remaining damage to your damage thresholds:
- Damage < Major Threshold → Graze. You mark one stress slot.
- Damage ≥ Major Threshold → Wound. You fill a wound slot.
- Damage ≥ Critical Threshold → Mortal wound. You fill the mortal wound slot.

If you should fill a wound slot, but have no free wound slots, take a Mortal wound instead.
If you should fill the mortal wound slot, but it is already filled, the character dies.

When you Break from a Graze, you must pass a Strength save (Difficulty 8), or take a Wound.
When you take a Mortal Wound, you must pass a Strength save (Difficulty 8), or become Dying.

A wound has a Severity value equal to the damage that caused it (after damage reduction).
With treatment and care, a wound's severity can be reduced. When it reaches 0 or less, the wound is healed and frees up its slot.

A wound has associated Conditions. 

# Conditions
Conditions can be taken from injuries, environmental effects, or other sources defined by the GM.
These can be Treated relatively easily, negating their effects.

## Stress
When you mark your last Stress slot, you become Vulnerable until you can clear at least one Stress.
When you should mark a Stress, but have no free slot, you mark an Exhaustion instead, and roll a Break save.
## Vulnerable
When you are Vulnerable, either from full stress, or a disadvantageous position, attacks rolls against you get an additional d12.
## Break
When you Break, pass the save defined by the source of Stress, or suffer that source’s consequence. If the source doesn’t define a save, the GM picks a fitting attribute and a Difficulty of 8.
## Exhaustion
You lose one stress slot for each level of Exhaustion, reducing your maximum stress capacity.
Exhaustion can occur as a result of a Break, or deprivation of rest, food, and basic needs.
## Incapacitated
You are unable to move or act.
## Impaired
Some of your dice are downgraded in size.
Wounds and situational disadvantage can cause impairment.
## Bleeding X
Mark X stress every Sequence you move or act. If you Break that way, pass a Strength save or fall unconscious.
## Dying
You can only crawl weakly and will die without help.

# Progression
## Attribute increase
When you fail a roll including one of your attribute dice, add one progress mark to that attribute, up to a maximum of 5. At 5 marks, you may use a rest action to try to improve your attribute die.
## Trait improvement
A trait can be improved or acquired through training, study, survival, achievement. Make sure to remind the GM, and as a GM, make sure to rewards players who focus on their strengths and growth.
## Max Stress increase
When you heal from a Mortal Wound, roll 2d6. If the sum is higher than your current maximum Stress, increase your maximum Stress by 1.

# Rest
In relative safety and comfort, you may take a rest.
## Short Rest
You may take one short rest per phase. It lasts about one hour. 
When you take a short rest, the GM gains 1d4 Omens.
During a short rest, you may perform two of the following actions:
- Clear Stress → Clear 1d4 Stress. You can use this on another character.
- Patch Wounds → Reduce the severity of 1 Wound by 1 or treat one of its conditions. You can use this on another character.
- Prepare → Gain 1 Resolve. (Gain 2 if you prepare with other allies)

An interrupted short rest yields no benefits.

## Long Rest
You may take one long rest per day. It lasts an entire phase (camping, eating, sleeping).
When you take a long rest, the GM gains X+1d4 Omens, where X is the number of player characters.
During a long rest, you may perform two of the following actions:
- Clear Stress → Clear all Stress. You can use this on another character.
- Clear Exhaustion → Remove 1 level of Exhaustion.
- Patch Wounds → Reduce the severity of 1 Wound by 1d4 (can be upgraded with a relevant Trait) and treat one of its conditions. You can use this on another character. 
- Prepare → Gain 1 Resolve. (Gain 2 if you prepare with other allies)
- Improve → If your attribute has 5 progression marks, roll its die. If it rolls its maximum value, it steps up to the next higher die size, up to d12, and you clear all marks for that attribute.
- Work on a project → Establish or continue working on a long-term Project.
- Scavenge/Forage/Scout → Search for useful items, food, or information. Roll your relevant dice to determine what and how much you find.

An interrupted long rest yields no benefits.
Spending 3 phases without a long rest inflicts 1 Exhaustion.

# Projects
During downtime, you may work on a project for a whole phase, once per phase or as a long rest action.
A project's Scale is represented by a die size. 
A project may require resources, materials or energy.
The project's Goal is that die size squared. (d4 → 16, d6 → 36, d8 → 64, d10 → 100, d12 → 144)
Every time you work on a project, make an Action roll with the relevant dice, with T=4. Then:
- On a success, add your result to the project's Progress.
- On a failure, subtract your result from the project's Progress — something goes wrong, wasting some progress.

When the Progress reaches the Goal, your project is complete.

Possible projects include:
- Crafting
- Building
- Research
- Training
- Teaching

# Fantasy Extension
Optional mechanics for magic enabled settings:
## The Cost of Magic
Magic drains the user, the bigger the spell, the higher the risk.
- When you make a spellcast roll, mark X-1 Stress for each group of X matching dice. For example: 
    - On a 6,4,4, mark 1 Stress (2 fours - 1).
    - On a 11,7,7,2,2,2, you mark 3 Stress (2 sevens - 1 + 3 twos - 1).
- When you Break that way, you must pass a Focus save or come down with **Arcane Fever**.
## Arcane Fever
You are unable to cast spells. You must spend 2 long rest actions to clear the condition.
## Concentration
When you cast a spell that requires continued attention, you must maintain Concentration.
- You may not Concentrate on two spells at once. Casting a new Concentration spell immediately ends your previous Concentration. Casting a regular, fire-and-forget spell does not break Concentration.
- When you take damage, you must pass a Focus save or lose Concentration.
- When you lose Concentration on a spell, its effect ends immediately.
## Casting speed
By default, a spell can't be cast as a reaction. Training and practice may allow it for specific techniques.
